Cadence is a productivity app for planning work and then actually doing it. It puts the pieces most people keep in separate apps into one place: a checklist and calendar for the day, projects broken down into subtasks, study sessions and flashcards, notes, habits, a focus timer, and reminders that keep at you until things are done.

Everything syncs between the browser and the desktop apps. It is built for people who want one tool they can grow into: it works with a mouse and buttons from the first day, and almost everything also has a keyboard shortcut once you want one.

Planning a day

A day in Cadence starts with one list. Tasks can happen once, repeat on a schedule, or stay on the list until you finish them. Items can have a time, a length and a deadline, and can be marked urgent so they show up today no matter when they're due. Your Google Calendar events and Calendly bookings appear alongside them, quietly, so the calendar shows the real shape of the day.

When you check something off, Cadence asks for a sentence or two about how it went. Those reflections build up into a record of how you work best, and they're searchable later.

Projects and studying

Bigger pieces of work go in Projects. Break a goal into subtasks, and those into smaller ones. Press Return for the next subtask and Tab to tuck it under the one above. Each project can be read as an outline, as a Gantt chart against the calendar, or as a map of the whole tree, and the dashed + boxes in each view add a subtask exactly where you want it.

The Study section runs timed rounds with breaks, or a sequence of intervals of your choosing, and tracks what you got wrong so the next session starts there. A no-computer mode notices if you reach for the keyboard when you meant to work on paper.

Reminders for procrastinators

Some reminders are easy to ignore. For the ones that matter, each task can be set to keep after you:

SettingWhat happens until you check it off
NormalOne reminder at the times you choose.
PersistentThe reminder comes back every few minutes.
PushyA window opens in the middle of the screen, again and again.
StrictThe window covers the screen, and putting it off asks you to say why. On the Mac, the apps and websites you list are blocked until it's done.

Keyboard and mouse

Nothing in Cadence requires a shortcut. There are buttons, menus and right-click options throughout. But if you like to keep your hands on the keyboard: single letters move between pages, ⌘Z undoes, ⌘C / ⌘X / ⌘V copy, cut and paste calendar items and subtasks, Tab indents, the arrow keys move between rows, and ⌘/ shows the full list wherever you are.

Your data, and Google

Connecting Google is optional. If you do, Cadence asks Google for permission to see and edit your calendar events and your Google Tasks, and uses it only for these features:

  • Show and import. Your Google events appear on Cadence's week and month calendars, and your Google Tasks on your list. They sync to your other devices and update when they change in Google; checking off a Google task in Cadence completes it in Google.
  • Find open time. Your busy times are used to show the time you have free over the next 7 days on the Booking page.
  • Create and edit on request. Cadence adds, edits, or deletes a Google event only when you ask it to, for example a new event set to sync with Google.

We never sell your data or use it for ads, and you can disconnect at any time. Cadence's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Read the details in our Privacy Policy.
