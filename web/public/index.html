Features

Everything you plan, in the place you do it

Most people keep a to-do list, a calendar, a timer and a notes app separately. Cadence puts them together, so a task you plan in the morning is the same task you time, check off and write about later.

One list for the day

Today shows overdue items first, then the day’s tasks and habits, then work you keep on the list until it is done. Your Google Calendar events and Calendly meetings appear next to them, and the week and month calendars show the same items by time.

  • Tasks can happen once, repeat on a schedule, or stay on the list until you finish them.
  • On the calendar, drag an item to move it, or drag its bottom edge to change its length.
  • Checking a task off asks for a short reflection on how it went.

Focus sessions that measure the work

Start a timer on one task, or pick a list of tasks and see how many you finish before the time runs out. Points come from the time you work and the tasks you finish, and staying focused for 90% of the session multiplies them by 1.5.

  • With no input for 5 minutes, or with the computer asleep, the session pauses, so time away is not counted.
  • On the Mac, the apps and websites you mark as distracting are blocked while a session runs.
  • Each task keeps the times it took, and Cadence estimates the next one from a weighted average of them.

Reminders that continue until the task is done

Each task can be set to keep reminding you. A persistent reminder comes back every few minutes. A pushy one opens a window in the middle of the screen. A strict one covers the screen and asks why you are putting it off, and on the Mac it blocks the apps and websites you chose until the task is checked off.

  • Reminders for things that are not tasks, such as drinking water every hour, are set separately.
  • Cadence checks in when you open or unlock your computer.

Projects and studying

A project is split into subtasks, and those into smaller ones, each with its own deadline. The same project can be read as an outline, a Gantt chart or a map. The Study page runs timed intervals, such as 25 minutes on and 5 off, and keeps flashcards that come back sooner when you miss them.

  • Subtasks due soon appear on the Today list by themselves.
  • A no-computer study session notes it when you use the computer during it.

The rest of the toolkit

Habits

Streaks, a 30-day grid, and a rule against missing twice in a row.

Reflections

Every reflection you write is kept and can be searched later.

Notes

Plain notes that sync with the rest of your account.

Bonus tasks

Optional tasks that earn extra points when you do them during a focus session.

Booking

Your open time for the next 7 days, worked out from your calendars.

Screen time

On the Mac, the time spent in each app, with a daily limit for distracting ones.

Flashcards

Cards you miss come back soon, and cards you know come back less often.

Sync

Changes reach your other devices within seconds, and the apps keep working offline.

How it works

A day in three steps

1

Plan

Add today’s tasks, set the ones that repeat, and connect your calendars so meetings appear on their own.

2

Do

Cadence reminds you at the times you set, times your focus sessions, and checks in when you come back to the computer.

3

Reflect

When you check something off, write a few words on how it went. Over time this shows what works for you.

Your data

How Cadence uses Google Calendar and Google Tasks

Connecting Google is optional. If you do, Cadence asks Google for permission to see and edit your calendar events and your Google Tasks, and uses it only for these features:

Show and import. Your Google events appear on Cadence's week and month calendars, and your Google Tasks on your list. They sync to your other devices and update when they change in Google; checking off a Google task in Cadence completes it in Google.
Find open time. Your busy times are used to show the time you have free over the next 7 days on the Booking page.
Create and edit on request. Cadence adds, edits, or deletes a Google event only when you ask it to, for example a new event set to sync with Google.

We never sell your data or use it for ads, and you can disconnect at any time. Cadence's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Read the details in our Privacy Policy.

Download

Cadence on each of your devices

The desktop apps keep reminders running in the background and check in when you open your computer. Sign in with the same account on each device and everything stays in sync.

Mac

macOS 14 or later, Apple Silicon or Intel.

Download for Mac On first launch, if macOS cannot verify it, open System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.

Windows

Windows 10 or 11. It runs in the system tray and starts with Windows.

Download for Windows If SmartScreen appears, click More info, then Run anyway.

Linux

An AppImage for any distribution, or a .deb for Debian and Ubuntu.

AppImage .deb
For the AppImage, run chmod +x Cadence.AppImage, then open it.

iPhone and Android

Use the web app from your home screen. It opens without the browser bars.

Open the web app On iPhone, tap Share in Safari, then Add to Home Screen. On Android, choose Install app in Chrome’s menu.

All releases are on GitHub.
